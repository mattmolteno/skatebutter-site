The short version

SkateButter has no account, no ads, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no SkateButter server. Your rides are stored on your phone. Some data can still leave it: showing the map tells the map provider roughly where you are, your phone's own backup may include your rides, and anything you export goes where you send it.

What the apps record

While you ride, the apps record your GPS position, the phone's motion sensors (accelerometer and gyroscope), your good/bad ratings and any tags you add. Location traces and movement data can reveal where you live and where you go, so we treat them as sensitive. The apps do not ask for microphone, camera or contacts access.

  • Android keeps these in ride files in the app's storage. Raw sensor logging for diagnostics is opt-in. Crash reports are written on the phone (device model, error details, recent app actions, no coordinates) and are not uploaded.
  • iPhone keeps ride data in the app's Documents folder. It also writes a raw 100 Hz motion and GPS file for every ride, with no opt-in yet. Deleting a route on iPhone does not delete that file, and there is not yet a way to delete these files from inside the app. Deleting the app removes them.

Deleting your data

  • Android "Clear history" removes measurements, labels and tags. It does not remove tap diagnostics files, tap calibration files or crash reports. Clearing the app's storage in Android settings or uninstalling removes them from the phone, but not any backup copy.
  • iPhone: see above. Deleting a route does not delete its raw sensor and GPS file.
  • Copies in a phone backup, or in an export you sent somewhere, are not removed by the app. Delete them where they are stored.

Example route

Both apps include a built-in demo route in Utrecht, and the Android map shows that area until your GPS position arrives. It is not your data.

This website

This site sets no cookies and runs no analytics or trackers, and loads no third-party fonts or scripts. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub keeps its own server logs (such as IP addresses) and may handle downloads of test builds from GitHub Releases under its privacy statement. The email form on the home page is not connected yet; when it is, this page will name the provider and what is stored.
